<div class="career-section-heading"><div><div class="career-eyebrow">OVERVIEW</div><h2>Why this matters</h2></div><p>Understand the context before drawing conclusions.</p></div>
<div class="skill-detail-panel"><p>Job postings provide a current snapshot of employer expectations. Titles can differ between organizations, so comparing responsibilities is often more useful than comparing titles alone.</p></div>

<div class="career-section-heading"><div><div class="career-eyebrow">RESEARCH LANDSCAPE</div><h2>What to capture</h2></div><p>Important concepts to investigate and compare.</p></div>

<div class="career-section-heading"><div><div class="career-eyebrow">PRACTICAL EXAMPLE</div><h2>How to use this resource</h2></div><p>Connect the topic to a concrete research question.</p></div>
<div class="skill-detail-panel skill-project"><div class="career-eyebrow">RESEARCH EXERCISE</div><p>Collect a small sample of postings for one role. Group recurring skills, distinguish required from preferred qualifications, and note the experience level and location of each listing.</p></div>

<div class="career-section-heading"><div><div class="career-eyebrow">RESEARCH METHOD</div><h2>How to investigate it</h2></div><p>Use a consistent method and keep track of evidence quality.</p></div>
<div class="skill-detail-panel"><p>A job posting describes one employer's needs at one point in time. It is not a complete measure of the labor market, and duplicated or expired listings can distort a sample.</p></div>
