<div class="career-section-heading"><div><div class="career-eyebrow">OVERVIEW</div><h2>Why this matters</h2></div><p>Understand the context before drawing conclusions.</p></div>
<div class="skill-detail-panel"><p>Industry reports can provide a broad view of business priorities and market developments. Their findings should be interpreted in light of the publisher, research method, sponsor, and sample.</p></div>

<div class="career-section-heading"><div><div class="career-eyebrow">PRACTICAL EXAMPLE</div><h2>How to use this resource</h2></div><p>Connect the topic to a concrete research question.</p></div>
<div class="skill-detail-panel skill-project"><div class="career-eyebrow">RESEARCH EXERCISE</div><p>Compare two reports addressing the same topic. Record their publication dates, geographic scope, sample sizes, definitions, and whether the authors disclose sponsors or limitations.</p></div>

<div class="career-section-heading"><div><div class="career-eyebrow">RESEARCH METHOD</div><h2>How to investigate it</h2></div><p>Use a consistent method and keep track of evidence quality.</p></div>
<div class="skill-detail-panel"><p>Industry reports may rely on surveys, forecasts, or vendor data. Label these evidence types clearly and avoid treating projections as observed outcomes.</p></div>
